# Plan the interface around its use

Use for a new or materially changed player-facing workflow. Scale the design work
to the decisions still open; a small correction to a settled view can proceed
directly. These are connected design considerations, not a questionnaire or a
mandatory approval stage.

## Choose an interaction and information model

Recover the player's goal, common route and task frequency from the brief and
existing product. Walk a representative task from entry to result, including a
mistake and recovery. Identify what the player must recognize, compare and predict
before acting, and what deserves persistent attention versus focused disclosure.

Choose the visual/interaction model for that work. Position or adjacency may be
best understood and changed in a spatial view; repeated attribute comparisons may
favor aligned rows, and focused configuration may suit a form. A polished card grid
can still obscure either relationship. Use the model that makes the important
choice legible and efficient; spatial controls and icons do not replace hierarchy,
explanations or accessible alternatives. Compare plausible alternatives against
the same task rather than treating a familiar component as the design.

Connect the view hierarchy to actions and consequences. Reuse a subject's view
across contexts when it serves the same task, with appropriate editing/authority
states. Make selection, inspection, draft changes, commitment, feedback and
recovery distinct where they differ. Consider supported input, accessibility and
responsive behavior while choosing the model; they can change its organization.

Resolve only consequential uncertainty through `grill`, with evidence, alternatives
and a recommendation. Reuse settled numbered answers. Own ordinary composition
and control choices within the accepted direction; escalate a contradiction, not
every design refinement.

## Make the intended result concrete

Before broad production wiring, produce an annotated visual design and a complete
task walkthrough at a fidelity proportional to the change. A sketch may resolve
organization, but empty boxes and control names do not specify the final look.
Use representative real content and the intended art, or identify an unresolved
asset choice and what it could change. Establish layout/proportions, focal emphasis,
typography, spacing/density and primary versus secondary actions. Show the material
selected, empty, invalid and read-only states, transitions and recovery, plus how
the composition changes at supported narrow sizes and large text.

The artifact should let an implementer build the central experience without
inventing it, and let a reviewer question its visual quality and task flow. Check
whether the frequent task is discoverable, important comparisons remain visible,
and a correction avoids unnecessary navigation or repeated editing. Resolve
material design findings; carry remaining uncertainty explicitly into the plan.
A reviewable artifact does not itself require another user approval. Reuse accepted
design work, and carry the chosen design and task-based acceptance into UI building
and verification through the available specialist skills.
